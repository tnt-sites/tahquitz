Routine exercise is an important part of a healthy body. Getting your heart pumping can support this organ and other facets of your life. That said, working out can put your smile at risk if you aren’t careful. It’s not the act itself, but rather habits that you may be doing during it. Here is what you should be aware of and tips to avoid [dental emergencies](/palm-springs-ca/emergency-dentistry/) along the way.

## The Impact of Exercising on Your Smile

First and foremost, exercise can be great for your oral health. It lowers your risk of gum disease because it boosts your immune system. However, certain habits can be problematic, such as:

- **Mouth breathing** may be what you need to catch your breath, but it leads to dry mouth. When you can’t produce enough saliva, bacteria can linger and attack your teeth and gums, leading to cavities, infections, and the like.
- **Clenching your teeth** during particularly hard reps can put your grin under excess pressure. This can cause chips and damage if you aren’t careful.
- **Sports drinks** can replenish your electrolytes, but they are acidic and sugary. The more you have them, the quicker they can erode your enamel and lead to decay and sensitivity. In as few as five consistent days, you can develop signs of wear.
- **Ignoring safety precautions** can be troublesome. You might not stick to them as much as you get used to your routine, but they are there for a reason. One mistake can lead to an injury to your body or face.

## How Can You Protect Your Smile?

Although your oral health can be affected by exercise, there are ways you can protect your teeth and gums. For instance, making a conscious effort to breathe in through your nose and out of your mouth can help you avoid dry mouth. This can also relax your jaw and improve your airflow. Water is always a safe option to drink, but coconut water is a great alternative. Both are gentle and hydrating. Sports drinks can be good, but have them in moderation. Don’t forget about safety measures, like wearing a mouthguard.

In the end, don’t forget to visit your dentist for checkups and cleanings. These visits are great ways to ensure your teeth and gums are in great shape. If anything is worrying, you can learn how to prevent harm. That way, you can keep working out and know your smile is protected.

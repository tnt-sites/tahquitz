The main goal of palate repair surgery is to close the gap and create a functional palate, helping the teeth and facial bones continue to develop normally. The surgical procedure is usually performed when the child is between nine and 18 months of age, and commonly includes specialists like an oral surgeon, pediatric dentist, orthodontist, or plastic surgeon.

    heading: "What is Involved in Palate Repair?"
    subtext: |-
      Palate repair surgery is a complex procedure that requires careful planning and execution by skilled experts, like the ones at Tahquitz Dental Group. Before the surgery, our experts perform a thorough evaluation to determine the extent of the cleft and any other associated medical conditions. The sooner the treatment begins – as early as when the child is six weeks old – the sooner the rest of their mouth develops properly.

      During the surgery, the child receives general anesthesia or sedation through an IV. The surgeon makes incisions on either side of the cleft and moves the tissue together to close the gap. The doctor may also use tissue from other areas of the body, such as the roof of the mouth or the back of the throat, to help close the gap and create a functional palate.

      After the surgery, it is recommended to monitor the child and continue with regular checkups at Tahquitz Dental Group. Necessary dental work may include bone grafts or orthodontic care to address misaligned teeth. Your dentist at Tahquitz Dental Group regularly examines the oral health of your child to keep up to date on the situation as the mouth grows and develops.
